#### Who I am

Nice to meet you, I'm Samuel (surprise, it's not just Sam). I'm a young, curious, and passionate developer. I'm mainly interested in low-level programming and algorithms, with a recent attraction to cybersecurity.

---

#### My values

I have values that reflect in computing, like open source (#communism).
More seriously, what I want is to share my knowledge and work (even if right now it's not amazing), with the ultimate goal of creating something useful for everyone... even if that "everyone" is just two geeks in their rooms—it's already something.

---
#### My journey

I really started computer science at the **IUT**, where we discovered so many things that even summarizing them is complicated.
It's also at the IUT that I discovered student life and, above all, associative life through **Amicale Core**, of which I became president for 1 year. During that time, we organized tons of events, including the integration of new students (it was *really* fun).

It was before my internship that I discovered cybersecurity through cryptography, and I really liked this field.
Then, I did an internship in a research lab and I loved the freedom and the topics covered (yes, I talked with researchers and it was super interesting). And for once, I did something other than C (some Python).

Then, I spent 1 year at **ÉTS in Montreal**. I joined robotics clubs (yep, still low-level), where I was introduced to Quebec culture, and we also worked a bit (here's the [link](/portfolio/en/commitments) to see what I did in the club).
On top of that, I also joined the **DCI (Division des Compétitions Informatique)**. I did about a dozen CTFs/hackathons with them, and that's where I continued working on crypto with a more applied focus on real systems.
I also discovered that crypto alone is really ineffective, and I discovered reverse engineering and other methods to gather information in order to finally find cryptographic vulnerabilities.
I also got to discover Quebec and Montreal universities by participating in competitions.
I'm not very good at CTFs or hackathons, unfortunately, but they're always great moments where you can meet lots of people and discover new tech or create fun algorithms.
