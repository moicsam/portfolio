### Project Overview

**Problem statement**: How can we generate trail graphs from GPS traces?  
When hiking, thousands of GPS traces (sequences of GPS coordinates) are created every day. These traces aren't random: they follow real paths. So the researchers asked themselves:  
*"How can we reconstruct the paths taken by hikers from all these traces?"*

This is the subject I worked on during my internship — **not the research side**, but the technical side of data management and **shaping it** for the AI.  
The goal? Find a **cheaper** method for obtaining up-to-date maps, without having to send teams out into the field to map the mountains.

---

### Achievements

#### Geographic database

My first task was to build a **database** with `PostgreSQL` to centralize and normalize data coming from `.geojson` or `.gpx` files (which is really just `XML` in disguise). I also wrote Python scripts to easily migrate data from files into the database, and to have an **easy** way to export it back to GeoJSON (death to `XML`, it's way too heavy for nothing).

### Contribution to an AI model

Most of my internship involved **assisting a master's student** in developing an AI model.  
My role was to handle the **pre- and post-processing of data**, because as the saying goes: *"Garbage in, garbage out."*

##### Key steps:
1. **Data cleaning**:
   - Removing points that were too far apart from each other.
   - Filtering out traces with too few points.

2. **Preparing data for the model**:
   - Creating **heatmaps** representing trace density per pixel.

3. **Generating training sets**:
   - Splitting the map into **fixed-size images** for training.

4. **Generating supervision sets**:
   - Retrieving **OpenStreetMap** (OSM) data via their API for the studied area.
   - Cleaning it to keep only the **paths covered by our traces**.
   - Converting it into a **raster bitmap** to serve as ground truth.

5. **Post-processing**:
   - After inference, **assembling the images** generated by the network.
   - Converting them back into **usable geographic data**.

---
### Results

**Data centralization**:
I simplified the storage of geographic data for my supervisor, who had previously kept it in files with **inconsistent, mismatched formats**.

**AI workflow optimization**:
My colleague was able to **focus on the core of the model** thanks to:
- A **simplified method** for generating datasets, **regardless of the source** (as long as OSM has traces).
- A **cleaner and more efficient** process than the old correction method.

**Model performance**:
Our pipeline made it possible to **train a model that outperformed the state of the art**, thanks to:
- **Clean and structured data**.
- A **fine-tuned model** built by us.
